🟦 Tile 1: Bounded Vector Fields — Poking the Tiles

* The Big Idea: Before you smash a structure, you give it a gentle nudge. This tool applies a tiny, restricted directional force to an AI's conceptual space.
* The Rule: It calculates a local "safety ceiling" (\(D\)). If your nudge is too hard, a built-in safety clamp steps in instantly, stopping the vector from shattering the local neighborhood!

🟩 Tile 2: Symmetric Lie Derivatives — Balancing the Warp

* The Big Idea: In pure physics and mathematics, if you warp a coordinate system, the grid lines must stretch smoothly and symmetrically. Non-symmetric twisting breaks the rules of smooth space!
* The Rule: This engine forces the math to obey index symmetry using a Lie Derivative. Even if you inject chaotic, twisted input data, the engine mathematically balances it out so the coordinates stay perfectly aligned without spinning out of control.

🟨 Tile 3: Gâteaux Variations — The Infinite Stress-Gauge

* The Big Idea: Traditional AI analysis uses rough shortcuts (like dividing vectors by vectors), which is mathematically illegal in multi-dimensional spaces! It's like trying to divide a compass direction by an airplane.
* The Rule: This tool fixes that by taking a strict directional calculus limit (\(\epsilon \to 0\)). It acts as an ultra-precise gauge, testing exactly how brittle or elastic a specific direction on the manifold is before it reaches a representation breakdown.

🟥 Tile 4: Path-Ordered Holonomy — Walking in Circles

* The Big Idea: If you walk in a perfect circle on a flat floor, you end up facing the exact same direction you started. But if you walk in a circle on a curved mountainside, your body tilts, angles, and shifts!
* The Rule: This engine sends a tracking marker on a complete loop (\(\gamma \)) through the AI's internal connection fields using a path-ordered matrix exponential (\(\mathcal{P}\exp\)). If the marker returns tilted or distorted, it exposes long-range semantic drift and structural asymmetry!
